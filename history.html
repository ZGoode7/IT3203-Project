The History of Web Browsers

How the browser developed from a simple document viewer into an essential tool for accessing the modern web.

Early beginnings

In 1989, Tim Berners-Lee proposed the World Wide Web at CERN. He later developed an early browser called WorldWideWeb, subsequently renamed Nexus. Early browsers were primarily focused on reading and navigating connected documents.

Evolution and competition

Mosaic, introduced in 1993, helped make browsing more accessible. Netscape Navigator gained popularity during the 1990s. Microsoft Internet Explorer then became a major competitor, contributing to a period often called the “browser wars.”

Modern browsers

Browsers now compete on speed, security, privacy, compatibility, and available features. People may choose different browsers depending on the devices and services they use.
